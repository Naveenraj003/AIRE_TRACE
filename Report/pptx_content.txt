AIRE-Trace: A Standardized Prospective Evaluation Protocol for Online Agent-Failure Warning
--- Slide 2 ---
Abstract
As autonomous AI agents execute multi-step tasks, they frequently encounter compounding errors that lead to failure. While recent research has proposed various online warning methods to detect these failures prospectively, these methods are evaluated under differing datasets, prefix definitions, calibration procedures, and evaluation constraints, making direct comparison difficult. AIRE-Trace introduces a standardized, prefix-only evaluation protocol that subjects different warning approaches to identical information, calibration, and evaluation constraints. By evaluating heuristic, classical, and sequence models under a common harness, this research isolates genuine predictive capability from evaluation artifacts, establishing a rigorous foundation for agent reliability.

Scope
3
Standardized Ingestion: Normalizing agent execution traces into a canonical schema for unified testing.
Leakage-Free Prefix Generation: Constructing outcome-independent execution prefixes to simulate real-time monitoring.
Cross-Method Evaluation: Assessing heuristic, classical machine-learning, and sequence models under identical constraints.
Ranking-Stability Analysis: Measuring how consistently methods rank against each other across different evaluation metrics.

--- Slide 4 ---
Objective
Primary Objective: To determine whether comparative performance claims for online agent-failure warning methods remain consistent when evaluated under a standardized prospective protocol.
Specific Objectives:
Enforce strict separation of training, calibration, and test data to prevent task-definition leakage.
Calibrate operational alert thresholds under a strictly controlled False-Alarm Rate (e.g., ≤ 5%).
Quantify method ranking stability using Pairwise Direction Consistency (PDC).

Problem Statement
AI agents can fail during multi-step execution, creating a critical need for early failure warning. While multiple warning approaches have been proposed, they are currently evaluated under varying conditions, meaning their reported performance cannot be compared fairly. A standardized evaluation protocol is urgently needed to evaluate different warning approaches under identical constraints to distinguish genuine predictive capability from evaluation artifacts.
Problem in one line: Reported performance claims for online agent-failure warning methods cannot be fairly compared due to a lack of standardized evaluation constraints.

Methodology
7
AIRE-Trace is not a new failure detector; it is a standardized prospective, prefix-only evaluation protocol for fairly comparing online agent-failure warning methods.
Evaluation Workflow:
AGENT TRACE → SANITIZE → PREFIX → CALIBRATE → PROSPECTIVE TEST → METRICS → STATISTICS → FAIR COMPARISON
Key Methodological Controls:
[Outcome-Independent Checkpoints] Evaluator receives no hindsight advantages.
[Terminal-Event Sanitization] Strict removal of outcome-bearing content from features.
[Prefix-Only Evaluation] Warning decisions use only information available in the observed prefix; future/post-onset information is not available to the prospective evaluator.
[Frozen Thresholds] Operational thresholds are frozen on an isolated calibration set.
[Episode-Balanced Weighting] Prevents long, noisy trajectories from dominating metrics.

Results & Discussion
Key Findings:
Random Forest (S1) achieved the highest observed AUROC of 0.6371.

PDC = 0.8294: Comparative method rankings are partially unstable depending on the chosen metric.

Conclusion
Standardized evaluation is necessary; AIRE-Trace provides a common prospective, prefix-only framework for fair comparison.
Discrimination alone does not guarantee useful early warning.
Because comparative method rankings shift depending on the evaluation metric, standardized testing is essential to isolate genuine predictive capability from methodological artifacts.
